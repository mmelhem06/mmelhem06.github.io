About

Building, testing, and checking the theory

I'm Mohammad, an aeronautical engineering student at Jordan University of Science and Technology. I'm drawn to aerodynamics: understanding how air moves around a body, and how to shape that body to make it faster, more efficient, or more stable.

I learn best by building, so I pair simulation and CAD with hands-on testing to see whether the theory holds up in the real world. I currently lead my university's Formula Student team and its Aerodynamics Department, alongside my own independent projects.

My goal is to work in aerospace R&D or Formula One aerodynamics, where design, analysis, and testing all meet.

CAD · Course project

Boeing 747-8 full-aircraft model

Recreated a complete 747-8 in Creo Parametric from public blueprints, working alone, in three weeks.

CourseAE204 Solid Modeling
ToolCreo Parametric
Duration29 Apr – 19 May 2026
ScopeFull assembly, 1:1
Drag to rotate · scroll or pinch to zoomExported from Creo as STEP

Approach

I picked the 747-8 for its difficulty: wing-body transitions, engine integration and a large assembly. I planned the work on a Gantt chart, then broke the aircraft into fuselage, wings, tail, engines and landing gear, each as its own part.

Key decisions

  • Built the fuselage from scaled blueprint cross-sections joined with boundary blends.
  • Boeing doesn't publish the 747-8's airfoils. My first two-section wing came out boxy, so I rebuilt it from three airfoil sections.
  • Used the Project tool to transfer an extruded outline onto the next section plane when the vertex counts didn't match.
  • Modeled the GEnx fan as one blade patterned 18 times at 20°.
  • Added windows and doors by projecting sketches onto the fuselage surface and offsetting them, techniques I learned beyond the course.
CFD + FEA · Independent

NACA 2412 wing: from lift to deflection

Coupled ANSYS Fluent and ANSYS Mechanical to find how far aerodynamic lift bends an aluminium wing.

Airspeed30 m/s
Lift (half-span)88.1 N
Max tip deflection0.117 mm
MaterialAl 6061-T6

Method

  • Built a half-span domain with a symmetry plane, which halves the cell count without losing accuracy.
  • Refined the mesh locally around the wing and ran a steady SST k-ω simulation at 30 m/s.
  • Linked Fluent to Static Structural in Workbench and imported the surface pressure as a distributed load, with the root fixed.

Result

The wing produced 88.1 N of lift and deflected 0.117 mm at the tip. That's tiny, as you'd expect for a solid aluminium section at this speed, and it confirms that the one-way coupled workflow runs end to end.

Research · AE201 team project

C-130 Hercules analysis & standard atmosphere model

A technical report on the C-130, plus a MATLAB model of the 1976 U.S. Standard Atmosphere.

Report41 pages
Model range0 – 105 km
Check at 20 kmwithin 1%
DatesDec 2025 – Jan 2026

Report

We covered the C-130's design, structures, propulsion, performance and cost. I led the sections on its history, evolution and modernization.

MATLAB model

  • Takes any altitude up to 105 km and returns temperature, pressure, density and speed of sound, with plots and Excel export.
  • I stored every layer's constants in one matrix and computed each layer's base temperature and pressure in a loop, instead of hard-coding them. Each altitude band then just picks its column.
  • At 20 km it gives 216.66 K and 0.0880 kg/m³, matching the standard table. Pressure is within 1%.

The core of the model

% Layer bases (m) and lapse rates (K/m), 1976 U.S. Standard Atmosphere
h_base     = [0, 11000, 25000, 47000, 53000, 79000, 90000, 105000];
lapse_rate = [-0.0065, 0, 0.003, 0, -0.0045, 0, 0.004, 0];

% Base temperature and pressure of each layer, built up from sea level
for i = 2:8
    T_base(i) = T_base(i-1) + lapse_rate(i-1)*(h_base(i)-h_base(i-1));
    a = lapse_rate(i-1);
    if a ~= 0   % gradient layer
        P_base(i) = P_base(i-1) * (T_base(i)/T_base(i-1))^(-g0/(a*R));
    else        % isothermal layer
        P_base(i) = P_base(i-1) * exp(-g0*(h_base(i)-h_base(i-1))/(R*T_base(i-1)));
    end
end

LayerData = [h_base; T_base; P_base; lapse_rate];   % one column per layer
